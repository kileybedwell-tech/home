# Letter 3 — CFPB complaint (hold in reserve)

**File at:** consumerfinance.gov → Submit a Complaint
**Do not file yet.** File only if:

- AeroPay does not respond substantively within 3 business days, **or**
- PrizePicks does not respond substantively within 5 business days, **or**
- either one declines to release the balance.

The CFPB routes the complaint to the company, which then has 15 days to respond
on the record. That on-the-record requirement is the actual lever — it is why
this works when support tickets don't.

**Category to choose:** money transfer / virtual currency / money service →
"Money transfer" → problem with the transfer, or funds not available when
promised. The complaint is about the *payment processor holding your funds*,
which is squarely within CFPB's remit; the sports-contest side of PrizePicks is
not, so frame it as a payments problem throughout.

---

## Complaint narrative

I am unable to access [$AMOUNT] held in my PrizePicks account. Since [DATE] I
have been unable to complete either a deposit or a withdrawal. The error shown
is "[EXACT TEXT]".

PrizePicks processes bank transfers through AeroPay. Because both deposits and
withdrawals are blocked simultaneously, the restriction appears to be at the
payment-processor level rather than a problem with any individual transaction.

I contacted AeroPay at support@aeropay.com on [DATE] requesting my account
status and the steps to reactivate. [Response received on DATE: "..." / No
response has been received.]

I contacted PrizePicks support on [DATE] requesting a written explanation of my
account status, my verification status, and any outstanding requirement on my
balance. [Response received on DATE: "..." / No response has been received.]

I have not been given a reason for the restriction, a requirement I could
satisfy to lift it, or a date by which my funds will be available.

I am requesting that my balance of [$AMOUNT] be released to me, and that I be
given a written explanation of why it was withheld.

## Also worth filing, in parallel

- **California Attorney General** consumer complaint (oag.ca.gov/report) — there
  is no California gaming regulator covering this, so the AG's consumer division
  is the state-level route.
- **BBB** — PrizePicks does respond to BBB complaints, and it costs nothing.

If the amount is large enough to matter and all of the above stalls, small
claims court in California handles up to $12,500 and does not require a lawyer.
